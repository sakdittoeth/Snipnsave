Snips

Save a passage while you're reading, and get back to the exact sentence later.

Download for Android

Android 8.0 or newer · free · no account

Installing it

  1. Tap the download button above on your phone, and pick the .apk file on the release page.
  2. Open the file once it has downloaded.
  3. Android will ask whether to allow installing apps from this source. Allow it, then tap Install.
That warning is normal. Android shows it for anything that didn't come from the Play Store. You're granting permission to whichever app opened the file — usually Chrome or your file manager — not to Snips.

Saving your first passage

Reading in a browser: select the text, then Share → Link to highlight → Save snip. The passage and a link straight back to it both come across.

Reading in the Substack app: select the text and tap Copy — its menu offers nothing else. Then open the post's share menu and choose Save snip. Snips puts the two halves together.

Open the post on its own page first. A passage shared from a feed produces a link to the feed rather than to the post, and "read in context" then has nowhere to return to. Snips tells you when that happens, and saves the passage anyway.

Where your snips live

On your phone, and nowhere else. There are no accounts and no analytics, and the only thing Snips sends over the network is a request for the title and cover of an article you saved yourself. It never stores the article — only the passage you chose.

That does mean there's no sync and no backup, so there's an Export in the menu that writes the whole library to a file you keep. Import reads it back.
